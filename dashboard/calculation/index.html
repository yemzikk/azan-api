7. The rule explained

What defines each prayer

PrayerBegins whenIn the calculation
SubhTrue dawn (fajr sadiq): light spreads along the eastern horizon.The sun is a set number of degrees below the horizon before sunrise. Methods differ on the number: 18° to 20°.
SunriseThe first edge of the sun appears. Subh ends.The sun's centre is 0.833° below the horizon: 0.567° because air bends the light, 0.267° for the sun's radius.
DhuhrThe sun has passed its highest point (zawal).Solar noon, plus a few minutes so the sun has clearly begun to decline.
AsrAn object's shadow equals its own length plus the shadow it had at noon. In the Hanafi school, twice its length plus the noon shadow.The sun's height at which that shadow length occurs.
MaghribThe sun's disc has completely set.The sun's centre is 0.833° below the horizon, plus a few minutes.
IshaTwilight has gone: the red glow in the Shafi'i school, the white glow in Abu Hanifa's view.The sun is a set number of degrees below the horizon after sunset: 15° to 18°. Some timetables use a fixed interval after Maghrib.

The rule the Assembly Constituency timetables follow

140 of the timetables, one for each Assembly constituency in Kerala, were produced by one consistent rule. It was recovered by fitting, then checked against all 307,440 of their published times: 99.4% agree to the minute and none differs by more than one minute.

PrayerAstronomical instantMinutes added
SubhSun 19° below the horizon−4
SunriseSun at −0.833°0
DhuhrSolar noon+3
AsrShafi'i shadow length+2
MaghribSun at −0.833°+3
IshaSun 17° below the horizon+6

Three further steps complete it:

  1. The sun's position is taken once per day, at 12:00 IST, and used for all six times.
  2. Each time is the latest value it reaches on that calendar date in any year from 2021 to 2099, so the time has entered whichever year the table is used in.
  3. 11 seconds are added and the result is rounded to the nearest minute.

Why Subh is "19° less 4 minutes" and not 20°

The method published for Samastha's timetables gives Subh at 20° and Isha at 18°. It also counts the gap between the true and visible horizons, together with the sun's radius, as one degree, "that is, 4 minutes". The timetables match that reading exactly: the last degree is a flat 4 minutes, not the 4.1 to 4.7 minutes the sun really takes at Kerala's latitude.

So Subh is the instant at 19° less 4 minutes, and Isha is the instant at 17° plus 4 minutes, with 2 more minutes of caution. At Kozhikode this is later than a true 20° by 5 seconds at the equinoxes and 43 seconds in June. Section 6 shows the true 20° beside it for any date.

What is sourced and what is inferred

  • Sourced: Subh 20°, Isha 18° on the red-glow criterion, Maghrib at the full disappearance of the disc, one degree counted as 4 minutes (Dr. Musthafa Darimi Nizami Karippur, Suprabhaatham, 18 May 2018).
  • Inferred from the data, with no published source found: the margins on Dhuhr (+3), Asr (+2) and Maghrib (+3); the extra 2 minutes on Isha; the 11 seconds; the span of years; taking the sun's position at noon.
  • Not applied in the timetables: height above sea level. Kalpetta's timetable follows the same sunrise and Maghrib rule as the coast.

What moves a prayer time

  • Longitude: 4 minutes per degree, 2.2 seconds per kilometre east or west, the same for every prayer and every day.
  • Latitude: depends on the season. A degree north (111 km) moves Subh and sunrise about 2 minutes one way in June and the other way in December, and hardly at all in March and September. Dhuhr does not change with latitude.
  • Twilight angle: each degree is 4.1 to 4.7 minutes. A timetable at 20° shows Subh 8 to 9 minutes before one at 18°.
  • Height: a viewpoint 100 m above its horizon sees sunrise about 1.5 minutes earlier and sunset 1.5 minutes later; at 780 m, about 4 minutes. Only height above the horizon you can see counts, not height above sea level.
  • The year: the same calendar date differs by up to half a minute from year to year, because the calendar and the sun fall out of step until a leap day corrects them.

8. How well each timetable is reproduced

All 191 source calendars, each compared over 366 days and six times a day. Choose a row to load it above.

The Assembly Constituency timetables follow the rule, and the point each was calculated for was recovered from the timetable. Every other calendar is only checked: the rule is applied at the town's map position with nothing adjusted, to see whether it fits.

A calendar that does not fit was made by a different rule; that is not a fault in it. Most of the older printed calendars also give one value for every three or five days, which a daily calculation cannot match on every day.

9. Questions for reviewers

  1. Are Dhuhr +3, Asr +2 and Maghrib +3 minutes the intended precautionary margins, and is Isha meant to carry 2 minutes beyond 18°?
  2. Is treating the last degree as a flat 4 minutes intended for Subh and Isha, or should a true 20° and 18° be used?
  3. Should a perpetual table take the latest time across the years, as these do, or should each year be calculated on its own?
  4. Should height be applied in Wayanad, Idukki and similar places? The source timetables do not apply it.
  5. Is one point per constituency acceptable, given that the east and west edges of a 30 km wide constituency differ by about one minute?
  6. Do any of the entries flagged as possible misprints in section 5 match the printed originals?
