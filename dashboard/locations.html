How to choose a timetable

Which timetable should I choose?

Follow the timetable your local masjid follows. If you are not sure, choose the one whose point is nearest to you. On the prayer times page, Use my location lists the nearest timetables with their distance.

Should I always choose my own constituency?

Not always. Each constituency timetable is calculated for one point in the constituency, shown as a small dot on the map above, not for the whole area. Constituency boundaries are drawn for elections, not for prayer times.

If you live near the edge of your constituency, the point of a neighbouring constituency can be closer to you than your own, and its timetable will match your place better. Choose the nearest point, even when it is across the boundary.

How much does the distance change the times?

Prayer times follow the sun from east to west: four minutes for each degree of longitude, which is about one minute for every 27 km you move east or west in Kerala. Dhuhr in Kasaragod is about eight minutes later than in Thiruvananthapuram all through the year.

Moving north or south changes Subh, sunrise, Maghrib and Isha only around June and December, by up to about a minute for every 60 km. In June, Maghrib in Kasaragod is about 15 minutes later than in Thiruvananthapuram; in December the two are the same.

Neighbouring constituency timetables usually differ by a minute or less. Within a few kilometres of a timetable's point, the times are the same to the minute.

Why do two timetables for the same town differ by a few minutes?

Calendars follow different rules: the sun angle used for Subh and Isha, the school followed for Asr (Shafi'i or Hanafi), and the few minutes of precaution (ihtiyat) added so that a prayer is never begun too early. Each is correct by its own method, so follow the calendar your masjid follows.

My calendar is missing, or a time looks wrong. What should I do?

Write to corrections@azantimes.in with the name of the calendar or place, and a photo of the printed calendar if you have one. Every report is checked against the printed calendar before a timetable is changed.
